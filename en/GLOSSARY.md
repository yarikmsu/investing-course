# Investment Terms Glossary

> **A comprehensive dictionary** of investment terminology for the US market.

---

## A

**Active Management** — An investment strategy where a manager attempts to beat the market by selecting individual stocks. Usually associated with higher fees.

**Asset Allocation** — Distribution of a portfolio among asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate). The main factor determining returns and risk.

**ADR (American Depositary Receipt)** — A certificate representing shares of a foreign company, traded on US exchanges.

**Annual Report** — A document containing a company's financial results for the year. Required for public companies.

---

## B

**Bear Market** — A market decline of 20%+ from peak. Characterized by pessimism and fear.

**Benchmark** — A standard for comparing investment results. For example, S&P 500 for US stocks.

**Bond** — A debt security. The issuer (company/government) agrees to pay interest (coupon) and return the principal at maturity.

**Broker** — An intermediary through which investors buy and sell securities. Examples: Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard.

**Bull Market** — A period of market growth of 20%+ from the bottom. Characterized by investor optimism.

**Buyback** — A company repurchasing its own shares from the market. Increases the value of remaining shares.

---

## C

**Capital Gain** — An increase in asset value. Realized when sold, unrealized while holding.

**Capital Gains Tax** — Tax on profit from selling investments. Short-term (under 1 year) taxed as ordinary income, long-term at preferential rates.

**Compound Interest** — Interest on interest. The main force of long-term investing. $10,000 at 7% for 30 years = $76,000+.

**Correction** — A market decline of 10-20% from peak. A normal part of market cycles.

**Cost Basis** — The purchase price of an asset (for calculating gains).

---

## D

**Diversification** — Spreading investments across different assets to reduce risk. "Don't put all your eggs in one basket."

**Dividend Yield** — Annual dividends ÷ stock price × 100%. For example, $3 dividend at $100 price = 3% yield.

**Dividends** — Part of a company's profit paid to shareholders. Can be ordinary or qualified (with preferential tax rate).

**Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)** — Regularly investing a fixed amount regardless of price. Reduces the impact of volatility.

**DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plan)** — Automatic reinvestment of dividends to purchase additional shares.

---

## E

**ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)** — A fund that trades on an exchange like a stock. Typically tracks an index with low fees.

**Expense Ratio (ER)** — A fund's annual fee, expressed as a percentage of assets. 0.03% = $3 per year for every $10,000.

---

## F

**Federal Reserve (Fed)** — The US central bank. Sets interest rates and regulates monetary policy.

**Fiduciary** — A financial advisor legally required to act in the client's best interest. Not all advisors are fiduciaries.

**FIFO (First In, First Out)** — Accounting method where the oldest shares are sold first.

**Fractional Shares** — The ability to buy part of a share. Allows investing any amount, even if a share costs $500+.

---

## H

**Hedge Fund** — A private investment fund for wealthy investors. Uses complex strategies, high fees.

---

## I

**Index** — An indicator reflecting changes in a group of securities. Examples: S&P 500, Dow Jones, NASDAQ.

**Index Fund** — A fund that passively tracks a specific index. Low fees, broad diversification.

**Inflation** — A rise in the general price level, reducing the purchasing power of money. Fed's target is 2% per year.

**IPO (Initial Public Offering)** — A company's first public stock offering. The company goes public on an exchange.

---

## L

**Limit Order** — An order to buy/sell at a specified or better price. Unlike a market order, may not execute.

**Liquidity** — The ability to quickly buy/sell an asset at market price. Apple stock is more liquid than real estate.

**Long-term Capital Gains** — Profit from selling an asset held for more than 1 year. Taxed at preferential rates (0/15/20%).

---

## M

**Margin** — Borrowing from a broker to buy securities. Increases both potential profit and losses. Not recommended for beginners.

**Market Cap (Market Capitalization)** — Total value of all company shares. Stock price × number of shares.
- Large-cap: >$10 billion
- Mid-cap: $2-10 billion
- Small-cap: <$2 billion

**Market Order** — An order for immediate buy/sell at the current market price.

**Mutual Fund** — A pool of investor money, professionally managed. Unlike ETFs, trades once daily at NAV.

---

## N

**NAV (Net Asset Value)** — Net value of fund assets ÷ number of shares. The price of a mutual fund at end of trading day.

**Net Worth** — Assets minus liabilities. Overall measure of financial position.

**Nominal vs Real Return** — Nominal is before inflation, real is after. 7% nominal - 2% inflation = 5% real.

---

## P

**Passive Investing** — A strategy of following an index without trying to beat it. Low fees, minimal trading. The Bogleheads philosophy.

**P/E (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)** — Stock price ÷ earnings per share. Shows how much investors are willing to pay for $1 of earnings.

**Portfolio** — The collection of an investor's investments: stocks, bonds, ETFs, and other assets.

---

## Q

**Qualified Dividends** — Dividends taxed at preferential rates (0%, 15%, 20%) instead of ordinary income tax rates.

---

## R

**Rebalancing** — Returning a portfolio to target proportions by selling appreciated and buying depreciated assets.

**Reinvestment** — Investing received dividends/interest back into investments to increase compound returns.

**Return/Yield** — Profit from an investment, expressed as a percentage. Includes price growth and received dividends/interest.

**Risk** — The possibility of losing some or all investments. High potential return is usually associated with high risk.

---

## S

**Sectors** — Groups of companies by industry: Technology, Healthcare, Financials, Consumer Discretionary, Energy, etc.

**Security** — A financial instrument: stocks, bonds, ETFs, options, etc.

**Short-term Capital Gains** — Profit from selling an asset held for less than 1 year. Taxed as ordinary income (up to 37%).

**Specific ID** — A method allowing you to choose specific lots to sell (for tax optimization).

**Spread** — The difference between buy (bid) and sell (ask) prices. Smaller spread = more liquid asset.

**Standard Deviation** — A measure of volatility. S&P 500 ≈ 15-20% annually. Shows how much returns can deviate from average.

**Stock/Share** — A security certifying ownership in a company. Gives the right to part of profits (dividends) and voting at shareholder meetings.

---

## T

**Tax-Loss Harvesting** — Selling losing positions to use losses for reducing taxes. Can deduct up to $3,000 in losses annually.

**Ticker Symbol** — A short designation for a security. Examples: AAPL (Apple), VTI (Vanguard Total Stock).

**Total Return** — Full return: price growth + received dividends. The correct way to evaluate investments.

---

## V

**Volatility** — A measure of price fluctuations. High volatility = high risk and high potential return.

---

## W

**Wash Sale** — A rule prohibiting deducting losses when buying the same asset within 30 days.

## Account Types

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Taxable Brokerage Account | Regular investment account without tax benefits |
| Traditional IRA | Contributions reduce taxable income, taxed on withdrawal |
| Roth IRA | Contributions from after-tax income, tax-free withdrawal |
| 401(k) | Employer retirement plan, often with matching |
| Roth 401(k) | 401(k) with after-tax contributions, like Roth IRA |
| HSA (Health Savings Account) | Triple tax advantage for medical expenses |
| 529 Plan | Education savings with tax benefits |
| SEP IRA | Retirement plan for self-employed, high limits |
| SIMPLE IRA | Simplified retirement plan for small businesses |

## Formulas

| Formula | Calculation | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Rule of 72 | 72 ÷ return = years to double | 72 ÷ 7% = ~10 years |
| Expense Ratio Impact | ER × portfolio = annual fee | 0.03% × $100,000 = $30 |
| 4% Rule | Portfolio × 4% = safe withdrawal | $1,000,000 × 4% = $40,000/year |
| Dividend Yield | Annual dividend ÷ price × 100% | $4 ÷ $100 = 4% |
| P/E Ratio | Price ÷ earnings per share | $150 ÷ $5 = 30 |
