## Research Reports

### SPIVA: Active Funds vs Indexes

```
Research: SPIVA U.S. Scorecard (S&P Dow Jones Indices)

Key data (as of 2023):
├── 87.98% of active funds underperformed S&P 500 over 15 years
├── 92.19% of active funds underperformed over 20 years
├── Similar pattern across other categories
└── Data updated twice per year

Source: spglobal.com/spdji/en/research-insights/spiva/
```

### Vanguard Research

```
Research: "The Case for Low-Cost Index-Fund Investing"

Key findings:
├── Low costs are the best predictor of future returns
├── Each 1% in fees = ~25% capital loss over 35 years
├── Active funds on average lag by their fee amount
└── Simple portfolios work as well as complex ones

Source: corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/research.html
```

```
Research: "Putting a value on your value"

On the value of financial planning:
├── Advisor's Alpha = ~3% additional return
├── Main contribution: behavioral coaching
├── Importance: asset allocation, rebalancing
└── Preventing emotional mistakes

Source: advisors.vanguard.com/insights/article/IWE_ReijsM
```

### Morningstar Research

```
Research: "Mind the Gap" (2023)

Behavioral costs for investors:
├── Average investor lags their funds by 1.7% per year
├── Reason: buying at peaks, selling at bottoms
├── Especially notable in volatile categories
└── Discipline and long-term focus are critical

Source: morningstar.com/lp/mind-the-gap
```

```
Research: "Predictive Power of Fees"

Impact of fees on results:
├── Expense ratio is the best predictor of future returns
├── Low costs win across all categories
├── Star ratings are less predictive than fees
└── Simple choice: take a cheap index fund

Source: morningstar.com/articles/752485/fund-fees-predict-future-success-or-failure
```

## Books and Authors

### John C. Bogle (1929-2019)

Founder of Vanguard, creator of the first index fund.

| Book | Year | Key Idea |
|------|------|----------|
| The Little Book of Common Sense Investing | 2007 | Index funds win |
| Common Sense on Mutual Funds | 1999 | Complete fund industry analysis |
| Enough: True Measures of Money, Business, and Life | 2008 | Philosophy of "enough" |

```
Key quotes:

"Don't look for the needle in the haystack. Just buy the haystack."

"In investing, you get what you don't pay for."

"Time is your friend; impulse is your enemy."
```

### Burton G. Malkiel

Princeton professor, author of efficient market theory.

| Book | Year | Key Idea |
|------|------|----------|
| A Random Walk Down Wall Street | 1973 (updated 2023) | Market is unpredictable, index it |

```
Key thesis:
"A blindfolded monkey throwing darts at a newspaper's financial pages
could select a portfolio that would do just as well as one carefully
selected by experts."
```

### William J. Bernstein

Neurologist and investment theorist.

| Book | Year | Key Idea |
|------|------|----------|
| The Four Pillars of Investing | 2002 | 4 pillars: theory, history, psychology, business |
| The Intelligent Asset Allocator | 2000 | Asset allocation |
| If You Can | 2014 | Brief guide for young investors |

```
The Four Pillars of Investing:
1. Theory — understanding risk and return
2. History — studying past crises
3. Psychology — controlling emotions
4. Business — understanding the finance industry
```

## Tax Data

### Retirement Account Limits

```
Source: IRS.gov

2026:
├── 401(k): $24,500 (+ $8,000 if 50+)
├── IRA: $7,500 (+ $1,100 if 50+)
├── HSA: $4,400 individual / $8,750 family
└── SEP IRA: lesser of $72,000 or 25% of income

Updates published October-November each year.
Link: irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-contributions
```

### Capital Gains Tax Rates

```
Source: IRS.gov

Long-Term Capital Gains (2026):
├── 0%: up to $49,450 (single) / $98,900 (married)
├── 15%: up to $545,500 (single) / $613,700 (married)
└── 20%: above these thresholds

Link: irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409
```

## How to Verify Information

```
Recommendations:
├── Check primary sources (SEC, IRS, official reports)
├── Look at publication date
├── Compare multiple sources
├── Be skeptical of "guaranteed" returns
└── Remember: past results ≠ future results

Red flags:
├── Promises of guaranteed high returns
├── No source citations
├── Pressure to decide quickly
├── "Secret" strategies
└── Too good to be true
```
