## Case 1: From $100 to $50,000 in 10 Years

### Investor Profile

```
Name: Alex
Age at start: 25 years
Income: $3,500/month (junior developer)
Starting capital: $100
Horizon: 10 years
```

### The Problem

```
Situation in 2014:
├── Just started first job
├── No savings except $100
├── Thought: "Not worth investing such a small amount"
├── Kept postponing: "When I save $10,000, then..."
└── Friends said: "$100 is not serious money"
```

### The Solution

```
What Alex did:
├── 1. Opened account at Fidelity (no minimum deposit)
├── 2. Bought $100 worth of VTI (fractional shares)
├── 3. Set up automatic transfer of $300/month
├── 4. Enabled DRIP (dividend reinvestment)
└── 5. Forgot the password to brokerage account (joking, but almost)

Strategy:
├── 100% VTI (Total Stock Market)
├── DCA: $300/month without skipping
├── Increased amount as salary grew
└── Never sold
```

### Result After 10 Years

```
January 2024:

Invested over 10 years:
├── Starting $100
├── + $300/month first 3 years = $10,800
├── + $500/month next 4 years = $24,000
├── + $700/month last 3 years = $25,200
└── TOTAL invested: $60,100

Portfolio value: ~$95,000

├── Net profit: ~$35,000 (+58%)
├── Average return: ~11% annually
├── Dividends reinvested: ~$4,500
└── Commissions over 10 years: ~$18

Path to $50,000:
├── Reached $50,000 mark in year 7
└── Growth accelerated thanks to compound interest
```

### Growth Visualization

```
Year   Invested   Value       Growth
────────────────────────────────────
2014   $100       $100        -
2015   $3,700     $4,100      +11%
2016   $7,300     $8,500      +16%
2017   $10,900    $14,200     +30%
2018   $14,500    $13,800     -5%  ← small drop
2019   $18,100    $22,500     +25%
2020   $21,700    $30,100     +39%
2021   $27,900    $45,200     +63%
2022   $36,100    $38,500     +7%  ← correction
2023   $48,700    $72,000     +48%
2024   $60,100    $95,000     +58%

$100 → $95,000 in 10 years of discipline
```

> ⚠️ **This is an illustration, not a forecast.** The year-by-year figures
> are a composite model, not a real track record. 2014–2024 was an unusually
> strong decade for US equities; the ~11% annual return shown here is neither
> normal nor a promise. Past returns don't guarantee future ones: forward-looking
> estimates of real (after-inflation) returns for the next 10 years are
> materially lower — roughly 4–6% per year (see the CAPE section in ADVANCED.md).
> The power of this story is the discipline and consistency, not any specific percentage.

### Key Lesson

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  "Don't wait for the perfect moment or a big sum.         │
│   Start with what you have. $100 today is more important  │
│   than $10,000 'someday later'."                          │
│                                                            │
│   The power of this story isn't the returns—               │
│   it's the DISCIPLINE: 120 months without skipping.       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Case 2: My 5 First-Year Mistakes

### Investor Profile

```
Name: Marina
Age at start: 32 years
Income: $6,000/month
Starting capital: $15,000
Goal: Learn to invest
```

### Mistake 1: Chasing the Hype

```
What happened:
├── January 2021: Everyone's talking about GameStop (GME)
├── Bought $3,000 at $320 per share
├── A week later: stock dropped to $50
└── Loss: $2,500 (-83%)

What I thought: "Everyone's making money, I'll catch it too!"
Reality: Arrived late to the party

Lesson: If everyone's talking about a stock—you're already late.
        FOMO (fear of missing out) is the investor's enemy.
```

### Mistake 2: No Emergency Fund

```
What happened:
├── Invested ALL spare money
├── Car broke down—needed $2,000 for repairs
├── Had to sell stocks at a loss
└── Loss: $800 (sold during a dip)

What I thought: "Money should be working!"
Reality: Forced selling = guaranteed loss

Lesson: Emergency fund of 3-6 months first, then investments.
```

### Mistake 3: Checking Portfolio Every Hour

```
What happened:
├── Installed 5 tracking apps
├── Checked prices every 30 minutes
├── Every 2% drop caused panic
├── Insomnia, stress, anxiety
└── Result: sold VOO during -7% correction

What I thought: "Need to watch the market!"
Reality: The market lives its own life, your nerves don't

Lesson: Invested—forget about it. Check once a month maximum.
```

### Mistake 4: Too Many Positions

```
What happened:
├── Bought 23 different stocks "for diversification"
├── Couldn't keep track of all of them
├── Commissions ate into profits
├── Didn't even know some of the companies
└── Result: chaos instead of a portfolio

What I thought: "More stocks = safer"
Reality: VTI = ~3,500 stocks in one purchase

Lesson: 2-4 ETFs is enough. Simplicity wins.
```

### Mistake 5: Investing Without a Plan

```
What happened:
├── Didn't know my goal (retirement? house? just because?)
├── Didn't know my horizon (5 years? 20 years?)
├── Didn't know my risk tolerance
├── Bought "whatever was recommended"
└── Result: random collection of assets

What I thought: "Main thing is to start, I'll figure it out later"
Reality: No plan = random decisions = losses

Lesson: Plan before first purchase, not after.
```

### First Year Results

```
Financial outcome:
├── Invested: $15,000
├── Value after one year: $11,200
├── Loss: $3,800 (-25%)
└── For comparison: S&P 500 that same year: +15%

Missed opportunity:
├── If I had just bought VTI: $17,250
└── Difference from reality: $6,050
```

### What I Changed in Year Two

```
New strategy:
├── 1. Created emergency fund of $12,000
├── 2. Made a plan: goal, horizon, risk
├── 3. Simplified portfolio: 80% VTI + 20% BND
├── 4. Deleted all apps except one
├── 5. Check portfolio once a month
├── 6. Automatic investments of $1,000/month
└── 7. Don't read financial news

Result of second year: +18% (vs index +16%)
```

### Key Lesson

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  "First-year mistakes cost me $6,000.                     │
│   But they would have cost $60,000 if I had made them     │
│   with more capital 10 years later.                       │
│                                                            │
│   Better to make mistakes with $15,000 than $150,000."    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Case 3: How I Survived the 2022 Crash

### Investor Profile

```
Name: David
Age: 45 years
Investment experience: 8 years
Portfolio size January 2022: $320,000
Allocation: 75% stocks / 25% bonds
```

### The Situation: Worst Year in a Decade

```
What happened in 2022:
├── S&P 500: -19.4%
├── NASDAQ: -33%
├── Bonds (BND): -13%
├── Even "safe" assets were falling
└── Worst year for 60/40 portfolio in 100 years

My portfolio:
├── January 2022: $320,000
├── October 2022 (bottom): $248,000
└── Paper loss: $72,000 (-22%)

$72,000—that's two years' salary.
```

### Emotional Roller Coaster

```
January:   "Small correction, let's buy the dip"
March:     "Hmm, already -10%, but it's temporary"
June:      "Inflation, rates... maybe sell some?"
September: "Down $60,000... this is a disaster"
October:   "It's all gone. Should have sold in January"

Voice of panic:
├── "Sell now before you lose everything"
├── "The market will never recover"
├── "You're an idiot for not getting out earlier"
└── "Crypto, gold, cash—anywhere but here"
```

### What I Did (and Didn't Do)

```
✅ WHAT I DID:

1. Remembered my plan
   └── Goal: retirement in 15 years
   └── Horizon allows surviving any correction

2. Looked at history
   ├── 2008: -37%, recovery in 4 years
   ├── 2020: -34%, recovery in 5 months
   └── Market has ALWAYS recovered

3. Continued investing
   ├── DCA $2,000/month—no skipping
   └── Bought the "sale"—stocks at 20% discount

4. Rebalanced portfolio
   ├── Sold some bonds
   └── Bought stocks at the lows

5. Tax-loss harvesting
   ├── Sold losing positions
   ├── Immediately bought similar ETFs
   └── Locked in $15,000 loss for taxes

❌ WHAT I DIDN'T DO:

├── Didn't sell in panic
├── Didn't try to time the bottom
├── Didn't change strategy
├── Didn't listen to YouTube "experts"
└── Didn't check portfolio every day
```

### The Result

```
Recovery:
├── October 2022 (bottom): $248,000
├── December 2023: $385,000
├── Growth from bottom: +55%
└── Growth from start of 2022: +20%

My purchases during the drop:
├── Invested $24,000 in 2022
├── Average purchase price: -15% from peak
├── That $24,000 is now worth: ~$35,000
└── Additional profit: $11,000

If I had sold in panic (October 2022):
├── Would have locked in $72,000 loss
├── Would have missed 55% growth
└── Would have lost ~$140,000 compared to current result
```

### What Helped Me Not Panic

```
1. Emergency fund
   └── 8 months of expenses—no need to sell

2. Understanding market history
   └── Crises happened, crises will happen, market grows

3. Automation
   └── Money invested without my involvement

4. Community support
   └── Bogleheads forum: "Stay the course"

5. Written plan
   └── "In case of 20%+ drop—rebalance and wait"
```

### Key Lesson

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  "The 2022 drop wasn't the first and won't be the last.   │
│   My portfolio survived 2018 (-6%), 2020 (-34%),          │
│   2022 (-22%)—and recovered every time.                   │
│                                                            │
│   The main threat isn't the market. It's MY emotions.     │
│   Those who sell in panic—lose money.                     │
│   Those who wait and buy—make money."                     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Case 4: Journey from Active Trading to Passive

### Investor Profile

```
Name: Steve
Age: 38 years
Profession: Financial analyst
Experience: 12 years in markets
Starting capital: $50,000
```

### Phase 1: Active Trader (2012-2016)

```
Strategy:
├── Technical analysis (charts, indicators)
├── 20-30 trades per month
├── Day trading and swing trading
├── Leverage up to 3x
└── Time on market: 3-4 hours per day

Typical day:
├── 6:00 AM — check premarket
├── 9:30 AM-4:00 PM — trading
├── 6:00 PM — analyze trades
└── 10:00 PM — prepare for tomorrow

What I thought: "I'm smarter than the market. Analysis gives me an edge."
```

### Active Trading Results

```
Year   My Return    S&P 500    Difference
─────────────────────────────────────────────
2012   +18%         +13%       +5%  ← "I'm a genius!"
2013   +12%         +30%       -18% ← "Bad luck"
2014   +8%          +11%       -3%
2015   -5%          -1%        -4%
2016   +6%          +10%       -4%
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Average: +7.8%      +12.6%     -4.8%

Plus:
├── Commissions: ~$8,000 over 5 years
├── Taxes (short-term gains): ~$12,000
├── Service subscriptions: ~$3,000
└── TOTAL hidden costs: $23,000

Real return after costs: ~4% annually

What if I had just bought VTI in 2012?
├── $50,000 → ~$95,000 by 2016
└── Without stress, without 4 hours per day
```

### The Turning Point

```
December 2016:

What happened:
├── Calculated real results over 5 years
├── Realized: S&P 500 beat me every year (except the first)
├── Counted time: 5,000+ hours on trading
├── Counted money: $23,000 on commissions and services
└── Read John Bogle's "The Little Book of Common Sense Investing"

Realization:
├── Market is efficient—I can't "beat" it consistently
├── Commissions and taxes eat into any advantage
├── Time is my main asset, and I'm wasting it
└── 85% of professional managers lose to the index
```

### Phase 2: Transition to Passive Investing (2017)

```
New strategy:
├── Sold all individual stocks
├── Bought 3 ETFs: VTI (70%), VXUS (15%), BND (15%)
├── Set up automatic purchases of $2,000/month
├── Deleted trading terminals
└── Cancelled all subscriptions

Time on investments:
├── Before: 3-4 hours/day = 1,000 hours/year
└── After: 30 minutes/month = 6 hours/year

Time freed up:
├── Professional development → 40% salary increase
├── More time with family
└── Hobbies and health
```

### Passive Strategy Results (2017-2024)

```
Year   My Return    S&P 500    Comment
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
2017   +20%         +19%       First stress-free year
2018   -5%          -6%        Market fell, I'm calm
2019   +28%         +29%       Great year
2020   +17%         +16%       Pandemic—held
2021   +24%         +27%       Continued DCA
2022   -16%         -19%       Downturn—kept buying
2023   +24%         +24%       Recovery
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Average: +13.1%     +12.9%     +0.2%

Portfolio:
├── 2017 start: $75,000
├── 2024: ~$280,000
├── Additional invested: $168,000
├── Capital growth: ~$37,000
└── Time spent: ~50 hours over 7 years

Comparison with first 5 years:
├── Active trading: 4% annually, 5000 hours
└── Passive: 13% annually, 50 hours
```

### Comparing Two Approaches

```
                        Active          Passive
────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Return (annually)       4%              13%
Time per year           1,000 hours     6 hours
Commissions             $1,600/year     $20/year
Stress                  High            Minimal
Taxes                   Maximum         Minimum
Odds of beating index   Low             Matches the index
                        (see below)     by design
Suitable for            Professionals   Everyone

Note: over long horizons the vast majority of active funds
trail the index — per S&P Global SPIVA (US, year-end 2024),
roughly 90% of active large-cap funds underperformed the
S&P 500 over 15 years. A passive portfolio doesn't "guarantee
success" — by design it matches the market's return (which has
been negative in real terms over some decades).
```

### Key Lesson

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  "I spent 5 years trying to beat the market.              │
│   Spent 5,000 hours and $23,000 on commissions.           │
│   And lost to simple VTI.                                  │
│                                                            │
│   Now I invest 30 minutes a month and beat                │
│   90% of active investors. Not because I'm smart—         │
│   but because I finally stopped getting in the way        │
│   of the market doing its job."                           │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Takeaways

### What Successful Investors Have in Common

```
1. DISCIPLINE
   └── Regular investments without skipping

2. SIMPLICITY
   └── 2-4 ETFs is enough

3. LONG-TERM THINKING
   └── 10+ year horizon

4. A PLAN
   └── Written strategy before starting

5. CALMNESS
   └── Don't sell in panic

6. AUTOMATION
   └── Remove emotions from the process
```

### Main Mistakes of Beginners

```
1. Waiting for the "right moment" or "enough money"
2. Chasing hype and hot stocks
3. No emergency fund
4. Constantly checking portfolio
5. Overly complex portfolio
6. Investing without a plan
7. Panic selling during drops
8. Trying to beat the market
```

### Formula for Success

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                            │
│   SUCCESS = Time × Discipline × Simplicity                 │
│                                                            │
│   • Time: start today, invest for 10+ years               │
│   • Discipline: every month, no exceptions                │
│   • Simplicity: VTI/VOO + maybe BND, that's it            │
│                                                            │
│   You don't need to be smarter than the market.           │
│   You need to be more patient than everyone else.         │
│                                                            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
