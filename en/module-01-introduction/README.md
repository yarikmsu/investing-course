## Lesson 1.1: Why the US Stock Market?

### Advantages of the US Market

The US stock market is the largest in the world by capitalization. Here are the main reasons why investors worldwide choose it:

#### 1. Size and Liquidity
- US market capitalization is over 50% of the global stock market
- High liquidity allows easy buying and selling
- Tight bid-ask spreads

#### 2. Access to World's Best Companies
- Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Tesla
- Companies with global presence
- Innovative technology leaders

#### 3. Transparency and Regulation
- Strict SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) oversight
- Mandatory company reporting
- Investor protection

#### 4. Historical Returns
- S&P 500 historically returns ~10% annually
- Long-term growth trend
- Recovery after every crisis

> ℹ️ **About that "~10%":** this is a **nominal** total return (with dividends reinvested) **before inflation**. The **real** (inflation-adjusted) long-run return has been about ~6–7% per year. It's a long-run **average** with wide year-to-year swings — not an expected or guaranteed return.

### Investment Risks

#### Market Risks
- Price volatility
- Economic crises
- Sector downturns

#### Currency Risks (for non-US investors)
- Dollar fluctuations
- Impact on returns in local currency

#### Regulatory Risks
- Changes in tax legislation
- Sanctions and restrictions
- Changes in market access

## Lesson 1.2: Basic Investment Concepts

### Key Terms

#### Stock (Share)
A security that represents ownership in a company and a claim on its profits.

```
Example: When you buy 1 Apple share, you become a co-owner
of the company (albeit a very small share).
```

#### Dividends
A portion of company profits distributed to shareholders.

```
Example: A company earned $1 billion in profit and decided
to pay $0.50 per share as a dividend.
```

#### Portfolio
The collection of all your investments.

#### Diversification
Spreading investments across different assets to reduce risk.

```
"Don't put all your eggs in one basket"
```

#### Market Capitalization (Market Cap)
The total market value of all a company's outstanding shares.

```
Market Cap = Share Price × Number of Shares

Example:
Share price = $150
Number of shares = 1 billion
Market Cap = $150 billion
```

### Company Classification by Size

| Category | Capitalization | Examples |
|----------|----------------|----------|
| Large Cap | > $10 billion | Apple, Microsoft, Amazon |
| Mid Cap | $2-10 billion | Medium-sized companies |
| Small Cap | < $2 billion | Smaller companies |

## Lesson 1.3: Risk and Return

### The Risk-Return Tradeoff

Basic investing principle: **Higher potential returns = Higher risk**

| Risk Level | Return | Example Instruments |
|------------|--------|---------------------|
| 🟢 **Low** | Low | Bank deposits, Treasury Bonds, Money Market Funds |
| 🟡 **Medium** | Medium | Corporate bonds, dividend stocks, index funds |
| 🔴 **High** | High | Growth stocks, tech companies, cryptocurrencies* |

> ℹ️ *Cryptocurrencies are a speculative asset with no intrinsic return grounded in cash flows: high risk **without** a justified expected return. They are not simply "another rung" of return alongside stocks, and crypto is not part of this course's recommended portfolios.

### Compound Interest — The Eighth Wonder of the World

Compound interest formula:

```
A = P × (1 + r)^n

Where:
A — Final amount
P — Initial capital
r — Annual return rate
n — Number of years
```

**Example (illustrative, not a forecast):**
```
Initial capital: $10,000
Return rate: IF 10% annually (not guaranteed)
Time period: 30 years

Result: $10,000 × (1.10)^30 = $174,494

Under this scenario, $10,000 would grow to $174,494.
```

> ℹ️ This illustrates how compounding works — it is not a promise of results. Real-world returns vary from year to year and can be higher or lower. The $174,494 is in **nominal** dollars (before inflation), so its purchasing power 30 years from now would be meaningfully lower.

### Rule of 72

Quick way to calculate how long to double your money:

```
Years to double = 72 / Annual return (%)

At 10% annually: 72 / 10 = 7.2 years
At 7% annually: 72 / 7 = 10.3 years
```
