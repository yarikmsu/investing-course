## W-8BEN Form

### What It Is and Why You Need It

```
W-8BEN — Certificate of Foreign Status

What it does:
├── Confirms your non-US resident status
├── Claims reduced tax rate under treaty
├── Avoids 24% backup withholding
└── Required by most brokers

Without W-8BEN:
└── Dividend tax: 30%

With W-8BEN (most countries):
└── Dividend tax: 15%
```

#### How to Find the Rate for Your Country


## Taxation for Non-Residents

### Dividends

```
Tax is withheld by broker automatically:
├── With W-8BEN: 15% (most countries)
├── Without W-8BEN: 30%
└── You receive dividends already minus tax

Example:
├── Dividends from VTI: $1,000
├── Withheld (15%): $150
├── Received in account: $850
└── This is final US tax
```

### Capital Gains

```
Good news for non-residents:

Capital gains are usually NOT taxed in US:
├── Sold ETF with $10,000 profit
├── US tax: $0
└── BUT: tax in your country of residence

Important:
├── This doesn't apply to US real estate profits
├── Check your country's rules
└── You need to declare income in your residence country
```

### Taxes in Your Country

```
Typical situation:

1. Dividends
├── US withheld 15%
├── Your country requires (for example) 20%
├── Credit for tax paid in US
└── Pay the difference: 5%

2. Capital gains
├── US: $0
├── Your country: according to local rules
└── Can be 0% - 30%+ depending on country

Recommendation:
└── Consult a tax professional in your country
```

## ⚠️ US Estate Tax for Non-Residents (Estate Tax) — Critically Important

> This is one of the most underestimated risks for non-residents who own
> US assets. Ignoring it can cost your heirs up to 40% of the capital.

### The Problem

The US imposes an estate tax on **assets located in the US (US-situs assets)**
that remain after the owner's death — regardless of where the owner lived.

```
For a US citizen/resident:
└── Exemption ~$15 million (2026) → almost no one pays

For a NON-RESIDENT alien (NRA):
├── Exemption is only $60,000
├── Tax rate: up to 40% on amounts above $60,000
└── The tax is paid by the HEIRS before they receive the assets
```

### What Counts as a "US Asset" (US-Situs)

```
Subject to US estate tax:
├── Stocks of US companies (Apple, Microsoft, etc.)
├── US-domiciled ETFs (VOO, VTI, VT, BND, QQQ)
└── Even if bought through a foreign broker!

NOT subject:
├── Irish/Luxembourg ETFs (UCITS): CSPX, VWRA, EIMI
├── Cash in a brokerage account (usually)
└── US Treasury bonds (under the portfolio interest rule — usually)
```

### Example

```
A non-resident (e.g., a resident of the UAE or Singapore) holds $500,000 in VOO.
The owner dies.

├── US-situs asset: $500,000
├── Exemption: $60,000
├── Taxable base: $440,000
├── US estate tax: ~$130,000-176,000 (progressive, up to 40%)
└── Heirs receive the asset only after the tax is paid

If the same money had been in the Irish CSPX (S&P 500):
└── US estate tax: $0 (not a US-situs asset)
```

> 💡 **This is why Irish ETFs are about more than just dividends.** The main reason
> experienced non-residents choose Irish UCITS funds (CSPX, VWRA) is protection
> from US estate tax, with the savings on dividend withholding being a bonus.

### How to Reduce the Risk

```
├── Use Irish/Luxembourg ETFs (UCITS) instead of US ETFs
├── Check whether your country has an estate tax treaty with the US
│   (treaty against double taxation of ESTATES) — there are few of them
│   (e.g., Germany, France, the UK, and Japan have one; most do not)
├── Avoid accumulating large amounts in individual US stocks / US ETFs in a personal account
└── For significant capital — consult an international tax attorney
```

> ⚠️ This is educational material, not tax advice. The rules are complex and
> depend on your country. Before making large investments, consult an
> international tax specialist.

## Irish ETFs — Detailed Section

### What Are Irish ETFs

```
Irish ETFs are funds registered in Ireland:
├── Trade on European exchanges (LSE, Xetra, Euronext)
├── Comply with European UCITS rules
├── Have tax treaty between Ireland and US
└── Especially beneficial for European investors

UCITS = Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities
└── European investor protection standard
```

### Benefits for Europeans and Non-US Residents

```
Tax efficiency:

US ETF (e.g., VOO):
├── Dividend: $100
├── US tax: 15% → $15 withheld
├── You receive: $85
└── Plus tax in your country (e.g., 13%) → minus another $11
    Total: $74

Irish ETF (e.g., CSPX):
├── Dividend: $100
├── Tax inside fund: 15% → $15 (Ireland-US)
├── Reinvested: $85 (no payment to you)
├── You don't receive dividend → no tax in your country
└── Tax only when selling years later
    Total: $85 working for you

Effect over 20 years:
├── US ETF: lose 26% of each dividend immediately
└── Irish ETF: lose 15%, rest grows tax-deferred
```

### Accumulating vs Distributing

```
Accumulating:
├── Dividends reinvested inside fund
├── No payments to investor
├── Maximum tax efficiency
├── Ticker usually has "Acc" or no suffix
└── Examples: CSPX, VWRA, EIMI

Distributing:
├── Dividends paid out to investor
├── Need to pay dividend tax
├── Ticker usually has "Dist" or "D"
└── Examples: VWRD, IEMM

Recommendation:
└── For accumulation, choose Accumulating
```

### US ETF vs Irish ETF — When to Choose Which

```
Choose US ETF (VOO, VTI) if:
├── You are a US resident
├── You don't have access to European exchanges
├── Investing through 401(k) or IRA
├── You need minimum expense ratio (0.03%)
└── Your country doesn't have treaty with Ireland

Choose Irish ETF (CSPX, VWRA) if:
├── You are a European or EU resident
├── Your country has high dividend tax
├── You want maximum tax deferral
├── You have access to LSE/Xetra through broker
├── Protection from US estate tax ($60,000 threshold for non-residents)
└── Investing for 10+ years (deferred tax)

Approximate effect over 20 years ($100,000, 2% dividends):
├── US ETF: lost to taxes ~$8,000-12,000
└── Irish ETF: lost ~$6,000 (internal tax)
    Difference: $2,000-6,000 in favor of Irish ETF
```

> **See the "US Estate Tax for Non-Residents" section above.**

## Common Questions

### Can I Open an IRA as a Non-Resident?

```
No. IRA requires:
├── Earned income in the US (wages, self-employment)
├── Social Security Number (SSN) or ITIN
└── US tax return

Alternative:
└── Pension accounts in your country + taxable account in US
```

### What If I Become a US Resident?

```
When moving to the US:
├── Notify broker of status change
├── Complete W-9 instead of W-8BEN
├── Get access to IRA, 401(k), etc.
├── Tax rules will change
└── Need consultation with tax advisor
```

### How to Avoid Double Taxation?

```
Credit mechanism:
├── US withheld 15% on dividends
├── Your country wants 20%
├── You declare income and tax paid
├── Credit 15% against your tax
└── Pay only the difference (5%)

Important:
├── Depends on tax treaty
├── Check your country's rules
└── Keep confirmation (Form 1042-S)
```
