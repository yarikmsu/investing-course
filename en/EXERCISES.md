## Exercise 1: Calculate Your Budget

**Goal:** Determine how much you can invest monthly.

### Fill in the table:

| **Free money** | $_______ |

### Questions to consider:

1. Can you invest 10% of income? ☐ Yes ☐ No
2. If not, what expenses can you reduce?
3. Do you have an emergency fund (3-6 months of expenses)?

```
50/30/20 Rule:
├── 50% — necessary expenses
├── 30% — wants and entertainment

├── Increase by 1% every 3 months
└── Automate transfers
```

## Exercise 3: Market Crash Scenario

**Goal:** Prepare for the emotional challenges of investing.

### Scenario:

```
You invested $50,000 in an ETF portfolio.
A year has passed, and the market dropped 35%.
Your portfolio is now worth $32,500.
You've "lost" $17,500.

News is frightening: "Worst crisis in 10 years!"
All your friends are selling stocks.
```

### What will you do?

Select an option and read the explanation:

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>A) Sell everything before I lose more</b></summary>

❌ **Worst choice.**

You'll lock in a $17,500 loss. If the market recovers (and historically it always has), you'll miss the growth.

Example: Those who sold in March 2020 during the 34% drop missed 100%+ growth over the next 18 months.

</details>

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>B) Do nothing, wait it out</b></summary>

✅ **Good choice.**

A long-term investor understands that drops are temporary. The S&P 500 has recovered from ALL historical crises.

Average recovery time: 1-3 years.

</details>

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>C) Buy more at low prices</b></summary>

✅ **Excellent choice (if you have spare cash).**

Buying during a drop = buying at a discount. Rebalancing requires buying depreciated assets.

"Buy when there's blood in the streets" — Baron Rothschild

</details>

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>D) Call my financial advisor in panic</b></summary>

⚠️ **Understandable reaction, but...**

A good advisor will say the same thing: hold, don't panic. Better to have a plan IN ADVANCE so you don't make decisions under stress.

</details>

### Write your "crisis plan":

```
If my portfolio drops 20%:
_________________________________

If my portfolio drops 40%:
_________________________________

I will NOT sell because:
_________________________________
```

## Exercise 4: Compound Interest Calculation

**Goal:** See the power of time in investing.

### Task 1: Who will accumulate more?

**Maria** started investing at 25, $300/month.
**Peter** started at 35, $500/month.

Both invest until 65 at 8% annually.

Who will have more at retirement?

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Show solution</b></summary>

**Maria (40 years of investing):**
- Invested: $300 × 12 × 40 = $144,000
- Accumulated: **$1,045,000**

**Peter (30 years of investing):**
- Invested: $500 × 12 × 30 = $180,000
- Accumulated: **$745,000**

**Conclusion:** Maria invested $36,000 LESS but accumulated $300,000 MORE!

Time matters more than contribution amount.

</details>

### Task 2: Calculate for yourself

Use the formula or calculator: [investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/compound-interest-calculator)

| Your Data | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Starting capital | $_______ |
| Monthly contribution | $_______ |
| Annual return (use 7%) | ___% |
| Investment horizon | ___ years |
| **Final amount** | $_______ |

### Rule of 72

Quick way to calculate how many years to double your capital:

```
Years to double = 72 ÷ Return(%)
```

Fill in the table:

| Return | Years to Double |
|--------|-----------------|
| 6% | 72 ÷ 6 = ___ years |
| 8% | 72 ÷ 8 = ___ years |
| 10% | 72 ÷ 10 = ___ years |
| 12% | 72 ÷ 12 = ___ years |

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Answers</b></summary>

| Return | Years to Double |
|--------|-----------------|
| 6% | 12 years |
| 8% | 9 years |
| 10% | 7.2 years |
| 12% | 6 years |

</details>

## Exercise 5: ETF Expense Comparison

**Goal:** Understand the impact of expense ratio on long-term results.

### Task:

You invest $100,000 for 30 years at 7% annual return.
Compare two options:

| ETF | Expense Ratio |
|-----|---------------|
| Cheap (VOO) | 0.03% |
| Expensive (active fund) | 1.00% |

### Calculate:

**With expense ratio 0.03%:**
Real return = 7% - 0.03% = 6.97%
Result after 30 years = $100,000 × (1.0697)^30 = $_______

**With expense ratio 1.00%:**
Real return = 7% - 1.00% = 6.00%
Result after 30 years = $100,000 × (1.0600)^30 = $_______

**Difference:** $_______

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Show solution</b></summary>

**Cheap ETF (0.03%):**
$100,000 × (1.0697)^30 = **$753,000**

**Expensive fund (1.00%):**
$100,000 × (1.0600)^30 = **$574,000**

**Difference: $179,000!**

That's almost **double the initial capital** lost to fees!

**Conclusion:** Expense ratio is critical. Choose ETFs with fees <0.10%.

</details>

## Exercise 6: Order Types (Module 2)

Match each situation to the right order type (market or limit).

| Situation | Your answer |
|-----------|-------------|
| Buying $500 of VOO in the middle of the trading day | ? |
| You only want to buy if the price drops to $450 | ? |
| Buying in the first minutes after the open (9:30) | ? |

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Answer</b></summary>

- **$500 of VOO mid-day → Market.** A liquid ETF, the spread is tiny, execution is instant.
- **Buy at $450 → Limit.** The order fills only at your price or better.
- **First minutes after the open → Limit** (or wait 15–30 minutes). Spreads are wider and prices jump at the open — a market order can fill at a bad price.

**Takeaway:** a long-term passive investor only needs a market order (liquid ETFs, mid-day) or a limit order. Stop-losses and take-profits are traders' tools.
</details>

## Exercise 7: Account Types and Taxes (Module 4)

Match each account to its tax treatment. Options: "after-tax", "pre-tax (deductible)", "taxed annually", "tax-deferred until withdrawal", "never taxed".

| Account | Contribution | Growth | Withdrawal |
|---------|--------------|--------|------------|
| Taxable | ? | ? | ? |
| Traditional IRA | ? | ? | ? |
| Roth IRA | ? | ? | ? |

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Answer</b></summary>

| Account | Contribution | Growth | Withdrawal |
|---------|--------------|--------|------------|
| **Taxable** | after-tax | dividends taxed annually | capital-gains tax only on sale |
| **Traditional IRA** | pre-tax (deductible\*) | tax-deferred until withdrawal | taxed as ordinary income |
| **Roth IRA** | after-tax | never taxed | never taxed (qualified withdrawal) |

\* the Traditional IRA deduction is not automatic — it depends on workplace-plan coverage and income.

**Takeaway:** Roth pays tax now, Traditional pays later. Expect a lower rate in retirement → Traditional; a higher rate or want flexibility → Roth.
</details>

## Exercise 8: DCA vs. Lump Sum (Module 5)

You have $12,000. Option A: invest it all at once. Option B: $1,000/month for 12 months. Which wins statistically, and when is B the sensible choice?

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Answer</b></summary>

- **Statistically A (lump sum) wins more often:** the market rises roughly 2 years out of 3, so "time in the market" usually beats waiting. Per Vanguard, lump-sum beat DCA about 2/3 of the time.
- **B (DCA) makes sense** if it's psychologically easier and reduces the risk of "buying at the very top."
- **Important:** investing monthly from your paycheck is NOT "DCA-ing a lump sum" — it's investing as the money arrives, which is the optimal approach.
</details>

## Exercise 9: First-Purchase Plan (Module 8)

Put the steps of a first purchase in the right order: **a)** buy an ETF · **b)** choose a broker · **c)** fund the account · **d)** open the account and fill out forms (W-8BEN for non-residents) · **e)** set up automatic purchases.

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Answer</b></summary>

**Order: b → d → c → a → e**

1. **Choose a broker** (Fidelity/Schwab for US residents; IBKR for non-residents).
2. **Open the account**, fill out forms (W-8BEN for non-residents).
3. **Fund the account** (ACH/wire).
4. **Buy a broad ETF**, ideally a marketable limit order mid-day.
   - US residents — VTI/VOO.
   - ⚠️ Non-residents: US-domiciled ETFs (VTI/VOO) are US-situs assets exposed to US estate tax (the $60,000 NRA threshold); Irish/Luxembourg UCITS ETFs are often used instead (see [NON-RESIDENTS](/investing-course/en/NON-RESIDENTS/)).
5. **Set up automatic purchases** — if your broker supports them for ETFs (e.g., Fidelity). Schwab's automatic investing is mutual-funds-only, so use an equivalent index fund or buy manually on a schedule.

**Takeaway:** the whole path takes about a week; after that, the main thing is to leave the portfolio alone.
</details>

## Investment Goal Simulator

**Goal:** Calculate the path to your financial goal.

### Step 1: Define your goal

| Parameter | Your Value |
|-----------|------------|
| Goal name | _________________ |
| Target amount | $____________ |
| Your current age | _____ years |
| Target age | _____ years |
| Investment horizon | _____ years |

### Step 2: Enter starting data

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Starting capital | $________ |
| Monthly contribution | $________ |
| Expected return | _____% (recommend 7%) |

### Step 3: Calculate result

Use the formula:

```
FV = PV × (1 + r)^n + PMT × [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r]

Where:
FV — future value
PV — starting capital
PMT — monthly contribution
r — monthly return (annual ÷ 12)
n — number of months
```

Or use an online calculator: [investor.gov/calculator](https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/compound-interest-calculator)

### Step 4: Results

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Final amount | $________ |
| Will you reach goal? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| If no, required contribution | $________ /month |

### Sample Calculations

<details>
<summary markdown="span"><b>Example 1: $1,000,000 Retirement</b></summary>

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Goal | Retirement |
| Target amount | $1,000,000 |

| Target age | 65 years |
| Horizon | 35 years |
| Starting capital | $10,000 |

**Required monthly contribution:** ~$550

**Calculation:**
- $10,000 grows to ~$107,000 ($10,000 × 1.07^35)
- Contributions ~$550/mo ≈ $6,600/yr for 35 years → ~$912,000
- Total: ~$912,000 (contributions) + ~$107,000 (starting) ≈ $1,019,000 — goal met ✅

## Investment Readiness Checklist

Before starting to invest, make sure:

### Financial Readiness

- [ ] Credit card debt paid off
- [ ] Emergency fund established (3-6 months of expenses)
- [ ] Stable source of income
- [ ] Investment money won't be needed for 5+ years
- [ ] This is NOT your last savings

### Knowledge

- [ ] Understand what stocks, bonds, and ETFs are
- [ ] Know the difference between Trading and Investing
- [ ] Understand that markets can drop 30-50%
- [ ] Know my risk profile
- [ ] Chose target asset allocation

### Plan

- [ ] Financial goals defined
- [ ] Required monthly contribution calculated
- [ ] Broker selected
- [ ] ETFs for portfolio selected
- [ ] "Crisis plan" created (what to do during a drop)

### If all items are checked — you're ready to start!
