## 2. DCA vs Lump Sum Investment

### How DCA (Dollar-Cost Averaging) Works

```
Example: $1,200 for a year

Lump sum in January:          DCA $100/month:
└── Bought 10 shares at $120  └── Buys each month

Month   Price   DCA buys
─────────────────────────────
Jan     $120    0.83 shares
Feb     $100    1.00 shares
Mar     $80     1.25 shares   ← more during drop
Apr     $90     1.11 shares
May     $110    0.91 shares
Jun     $130    0.77 shares
Jul     $140    0.71 shares
Aug     $120    0.83 shares
Sep     $100    1.00 shares
Oct     $110    0.91 shares
Nov     $120    0.83 shares
Dec     $130    0.77 shares
─────────────────────────────
Total:          10.92 shares

Lump sum: 10.00 shares × $130 = $1,300
DCA:      10.92 shares × $130 = $1,420

DCA won by +$120 (+9%) thanks to buying during drops!
```

### When DCA is Better, When Worse

```
DCA BETTER in falling or volatile market:

Price
$150 ┤
     │    ╭─╮
$120 ┤───╯   ╰───╮
     │           │    ╭───
$90  ┤           ╰───╯
     │
$60  ┼─────────────────────
     Jan  Mar  May  Jul  Sep

DCA buys more shares at lows


Lump sum BETTER in rising market:

Price
$150 ┤              ╭────
     │          ╭───╯
$120 ┤      ╭───╯
     │  ╭───╯
$90  ┤──╯
     │
$60  ┼─────────────────────
     Jan  Mar  May  Jul  Sep

Lump sum captures all the growth
```

### Historically: Lump Sum vs DCA

```
Vanguard study (1926-2019):

In 68% of cases lump sum beat DCA
In 32% of cases DCA won

BUT! Average difference: ~2% per year

Conclusion:
├── If you have money now → invest now
├── If not → DCA from salary
└── DCA is better for psychology (less stress)
```

> 📖 Source: [Vanguard "Dollar-cost averaging just means taking risk later" (2012)](https://static.vgcontent.info/crp/intl/auw/docs/resources/Dollar_cost_averaging.pdf)

## 5. Diversification and Risk

### How Diversification Reduces Risk

```
Number of stocks vs Risk:

Risk
(volatility)
     │
100% ┤█
     │█
 80% ┤██
     │███
 60% ┤████
     │██████
 40% ┤████████████
     │████████████████████
 20% ┤████████████████████████████████████████ ← market risk
     │                                         (cannot eliminate)
  0% ┼───────────────────────────────────────────

├── 1 stock = huge risk
```

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
```

### Long-Term vs Short-Term Taxes

```
$10,000 profit — how much remains after taxes?

SHORT-TERM (< 1 year):
├── Taxed as ordinary income
├── Rate 22-37% (depends on income)
├── At 24%: $10,000 - $2,400 = $7,600
│   ████████████████████████████░░░░░░░░░ $7,600

LONG-TERM (> 1 year):
├── Special rate
├── 0%, 15% or 20% (depends on income)
├── At 15%: $10,000 - $1,500 = $8,500
│   ██████████████████████████████████░░░ $8,500

```

### Tax-Loss Harvesting

```
How it works:

January: Bought VTI for $10,000
June:    VTI worth $8,500 (loss $1,500)

STEP 1: Sell VTI at $1,500 loss
STEP 2: Immediately buy ITOT (similar ETF)
STEP 3: $1,500 loss reduces taxes

Tax savings (at 24% rate):
$1,500 × 24% = $360 savings

⚠️ Important: cannot buy "substantially identical"
   asset 30 days before or after (Wash Sale Rule)

VTI → ITOT ✓ (different providers, allowed)
VTI → VTI  ✗ (identical asset, prohibited)
```

## Interactive Calculators

### Compound Interest Calculator (formula)

```
Future Value = P × (1 + r)^n

P = starting amount
r = annual return (decimal)
n = number of years

Example:
$10,000 × (1 + 0.10)^20 = $67,275
```

### Regular Investment Calculator (formula)

```
Future Value = PMT × [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r]

PMT = monthly contribution
r = monthly return (annual / 12)
n = number of months

Example ($500/mo, 10%, 20 years):
$500 × [((1 + 0.00833)^240 - 1) / 0.00833] = $382,846
```
