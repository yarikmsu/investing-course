# FAQ: 25 Key Questions About Investing in the US

---

## Fears and Doubts

### 1. Will I lose all my money?

**Short answer:** With diversification — extremely unlikely.

```
You CAN lose EVERYTHING if:
├── You put everything in one company that goes bankrupt
├── You use leverage and get a margin call
└── You invest in fraudulent schemes

You CANNOT lose everything if:
├── You invest in total market ETFs (VTI, VOO)
├── You don't use leverage
└── You use regulated brokers

Fact: In 150 years of S&P 500 history, it has NEVER gone to zero.
Even in the worst crisis (2008-2009) it dropped ~57%, then recovered.
```

---

### 2. Is now a bad time to invest?

**Short answer:** No. The best time is always "now."

```
Schwab study "Does Market Timing Work?":
Five investors each put $2,000/year into the S&P 500 over a
20-year window (results in DOLLARS, not annualized %):
├── Perfect timing (buys each year's low): finishes 1st
├── Invests immediately on the first trading day: close 2nd
├── Dollar-cost averaging monthly: 3rd
├── Worst timing (buys each year's high): 4th
└── Stays in cash / T-bills: last

Takeaway: investing immediately captures ~92% of perfect timing's
gain, and even the worst timer beats staying in cash.
(2005-2024 edition: $186,077 vs $170,555 — a gap of just $15,522.)

"Time in the market beats timing the market"

While you're waiting for the "best moment":
├── The market might rise 20%
├── You'll miss dividends
└── Psychologically even harder to enter
```

---

### 3. Do I have too little money to start?

**Short answer:** No. You can start with $1.

```
Modern brokers allow:
├── Buying fractional shares
├── Starting with any amount
└── Investing with no minimum deposit

Example:
$100/month × 30 years × 10% annually = $227,000

The main thing — start and do it regularly.
```

---

### 4. I don't understand finance at all, can I still invest?

**Short answer:** Yes, you don't need a PhD for passive investing.

```
All you need to know:
├── ETF — a basket of stocks in one purchase
├── Diversification — don't put eggs in one basket
├── Long-term — invest for 10+ years
└── Regularity — same amount every month

That's literally it. The rest is pleasant details.
```

---

### 5. What if my broker goes bankrupt?

**Short answer:** Your assets are protected.

```
Protection in the US:
├── SIPC insures up to $500,000 (including $250,000 cash)
├── Assets are held separately from broker's funds
└── In bankruptcy — transferred to another broker

Major brokers (Fidelity, Schwab, IBKR):
├── Have existed for decades
├── Manage trillions of dollars
└── Regulated by SEC and FINRA

⚠️ Cryptocurrencies are NOT protected by SIPC!
```

---

### 6. The market is at all-time highs — isn't it too late to enter?

**Short answer:** The market is often at highs — that's normal.

```
Fact: S&P 500 spends ~30% of its time at all-time highs.

If you had waited for a "correction":
├── In 2013 the market was "too high" — grew another 300%
├── In 2017 the market was "too high" — grew another 100%
├── In 2021 the market was "too high" — yes it fell, then grew again

Long-term trend is growth. Enter and stay in.
```

## Practical Questions

**Short answer:** $100-500 for your first purchase.

```
Optimal plan:
├── First purchase: $500 (to "get a feel")
├── Monthly: $100-500 (or more)
└── With each raise: increase contributions

Don't wait for a "big sum" — start small.
```

### 9. What should I buy first?

**Short answer:** VTI or VOO.

```
Why VTI:
├── Entire US market (~3,500 companies)
├── Expense ratio: 0.03%
├── Maximum diversification
└── One ETF — and you're in the game

Why VOO:

└── But results are almost identical

Choosing between VTI and VOO is like choosing between

```

### 10. How often should I check my portfolio?

**Short answer:** Once a month is enough. Once a quarter is ideal.

```
❌ Bad: Every day
├── Stress
├── Temptation to do something
└── Emotional decisions

✓ Good: Once a month
├── Check that automation is working
├── Look at the overall picture
└── No more than 5 minutes

✓ Ideal: Once a quarter
├── Assess if rebalancing is needed
└── Calmly and without emotions
```

---

### 11. When should I sell?

**Short answer:** Almost never.

```
Sell only if:
├── You've reached a goal (retirement, house purchase)
├── Rebalancing is needed
├── You critically need the money
└── Life circumstances have changed

DON'T sell if:
├── The market dropped
├── An "expert" predicted a crash
├── You're bored
└── You want to "lock in profits"
```

### 12. What should I do if the market drops 20%? 40%?

**Short answer:** Nothing. Or buy more.

```
At 20% drop:
├── This is normal, happens every 3-4 years
├── Continue regular purchases
├── If you have extra money — buy more
└── DON'T sell

At 40% drop:
├── Rare, but happens (2008, 2020)
├── Emotionally difficult, but...
├── This is the best opportunity to buy
├── History shows: market recovers
└── DON'T sell

Rule: The bigger the drop — the more you should buy.
```

## Taxes

### 13. What taxes will I pay?

```
US Residents:
├── Dividends: 0-20% (depends on income)
├── Long-term gains (>1 year): 0-20%
├── Short-term gains (1 year or less): up to 37%
├── High earners (MAGI > $200k single / $250k MFJ) also owe the
│   3.8% NIIT on investment income — effective top rates become
│   ~23.8% (long-term) and ~40.8% (short-term)
└── Use IRA/Roth IRA for tax benefits!

Non-US Residents:
├── Dividends: 30% (or 10-15% with W-8BEN)
├── Capital gains: 0% in US
│   Caveats: this 0% applies only to portfolio gains of a true
│   non-resident; it does NOT cover US real-property gains (FIRPTA)
│   or NRAs present 183+ days/year.
├── US ESTATE tax on US-situs assets (US stocks / US ETFs) above a
│   $60,000 threshold — consider Irish-domiciled (UCITS) ETFs
└── And tax in your country!
```

> ⚠️ This is educational material, not tax advice.
> Verify current rates and thresholds on IRS.gov.
>

### 14. Do I need an accountant?

**Short answer:** To start — no.

```
Not needed if:
├── You only have ETFs in a regular account
├── You don't actively sell
└── Your situation is simple

Needed if:
├── Complex tax situation

└── You're unsure about something
```

### 15. What is W-8BEN and why should I complete it?

```
W-8BEN = form for non-US residents

Why:
├── Confirms you are NOT a US resident
├── Reduces dividend tax (from 30% to 10-15%)
├── Confirms eligibility for tax treaty benefits
└── Valid for 3 years, then renew

⚠️ Complete it IMMEDIATELY when opening account!
Otherwise broker will withhold 30% from dividends.
```

### 16. How do I report in my country?

```
General principles:
├── Declare investment income
├── Account for tax credit (already paid in US)
├── Keep all documents from broker
└── Consult with local specialist

Broker usually provides:
├── Annual dividend report
├── Sales report (if any)
└── Confirmation of withheld taxes
```

### 17. What is tax-loss harvesting?

```
Tax reduction strategy:
├── Sell a losing position
├── Lock in the loss for taxes
├── Buy a similar (but not identical!) asset
└── Loss reduces taxable income

Example:
├── $5,000 loss on one ETF
├── $10,000 profit on other assets
├── Pay tax on $5,000 instead of $10,000
└── Savings: ~$750-1,500

⚠️ Wash sale rule: cannot buy a substantially identical security
   30 days before or after the sale (a 61-day window).
   This includes purchases in your IRA/Roth or a spouse's account,
   and DRIP reinvestments — pause DRIP on a fund you are tax-loss
   harvesting. Use a similar-but-not-identical ETF (e.g., VOO ↔ VTI)
   for the replacement.
```

## Strategy

### 18. ETFs or individual stocks?

**Short answer:** ETFs for 95% of investors.

```
ETF:
├── Automatic diversification
├── Low fees
├── No need to analyze companies
├── Statistically better results
└── Suitable for everyone

Individual stocks:
├── Requires analysis and time
├── Higher risk
├── Most underperform the index
├── For experienced investors
└── Maximum 5-10% of portfolio for "play"
```

---

### 19. How many different ETFs do I need in my portfolio?

**Short answer:** 1-4 is enough.

```
1 ETF (ultra-simple):
└── VTI — entire US market

2 ETFs (simple):
├── VTI (80%) — stocks
└── BND (20%) — bonds

3 ETFs (standard):
├── VTI (60%) — US stocks
├── VXUS (20%) — international
└── BND (20%) — bonds

4+ ETFs:
└── Complexity without significant benefit

More ETFs ≠ better. Simplicity is an advantage.
```

### 21. When will I become rich?

**Short answer:** Depends on your definition of "rich" and your plan.

```
$500/month at 10% annually:
├── After 10 years: ~$100,000
├── After 20 years: ~$380,000
├── After 30 years: ~$1,000,000

Accelerators:
```

### 22. Can I beat the market myself?

**Short answer:** Statistically — no.

```
Data over 15 years (S&P Dow Jones):
├── 90% of active funds underperformed the index
├── 95% of individual traders lose
└── Even professionals usually lose

Why:
├── Fees eat into profits
├── Emotions interfere
├── Nobody predicts the future
└── Market has already priced in all information

Conclusion: Don't try. Just buy the index.
```

### 23. What's "enough" for retirement?

```
The 4% Rule:
├── You can spend 4% of portfolio per year
├── Portfolio won't run out for 30+ years
└── That's ~$40,000/year from $1,000,000

How much you need:
├── Annual expenses × 25 = target portfolio
├── $50,000/year × 25 = $1,250,000
├── $80,000/year × 25 = $2,000,000
├── $100,000/year × 25 = $2,500,000

+ Social Security (for US residents)
+ Other income sources
```
