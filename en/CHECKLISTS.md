## 1. Investment Readiness Checklist

> Complete this checklist BEFORE starting to invest

### Financial Foundation

```
□ No credit card debt (or rate < 10%)
□ No other high-interest debt (> 7%)
□ Emergency fund established
   □ Minimum 3 months of expenses
   □ Ideally 6 months of expenses
   □ Money in a high-yield savings account
□ Have a stable source of income
□ Expenses < income (positive cash flow)
```

### Knowledge and Understanding

```
□ Understand what stocks and bonds are
□ Understand what ETFs and index funds are
□ Know the difference between active and passive investing
□ Understand the concept of diversification
□ Understand that investing is long-term (10+ years)
□ Ready for temporary portfolio drops of 20-50%
□ Understand that past performance doesn't guarantee future results
```

### Goals and Plan

```
□ Main investment goal defined:
   □ Retirement
   □ Financial independence
   □ Major purchase (house, education)
   □ Other: ________________
□ Investment horizon defined: ____ years
□ Regular investment amount defined: $____/month
□ Understand my risk tolerance:
   □ Aggressive (80%+ stocks)
   □ Moderate (60-80% stocks)
   □ Conservative (< 60% stocks)
```

### Final Check

```
□ All "Financial Foundation" items completed
□ All "Knowledge" items completed
□ All "Goals and Plan" items filled in

→ If YES — you're ready to open a brokerage account!
→ If NO — close the gaps first
```

## 2. Brokerage Account Opening Checklist

### Choosing a Broker

```
For US Residents:
□ Fidelity (recommended for beginners)
□ Charles Schwab
□ Vanguard
□ Other: ________________

For Non-Residents:
□ Interactive Brokers (recommended)
□ Other: ________________

Selection criteria (verify):
□ No commission for ETF trades
□ No minimum deposit (or acceptable)
□ Fractional shares available
□ Convenient mobile app
□ Good reviews and reliability
```

### Documents for Opening

```
□ Passport or driver's license
□ SSN (for US residents)
□ Proof of address
□ Bank account for funding
□ Email address
□ Phone number
```

### Opening Process

```
□ Went to broker's website
□ Clicked "Open Account"
□ Selected account type:
   □ Individual Brokerage (standard)
   □ Roth IRA (if eligible)
   □ Traditional IRA (if eligible)
   □ 401(k) rollover (if applicable)
□ Filled in personal information
□ Uploaded documents
□ Completed verification
□ Received account opening confirmation
```

### After Opening the Account

```
□ Linked bank account for transfers
□ Set up two-factor authentication (2FA)
□ Saved login and password in a secure place
□ Made first transfer to account
□ Waited for funds to settle (1-3 days)
```

### For Non-US Residents (additional)

```
□ Completed W-8BEN form
□ Indicated correct country of tax residence
□ Verified applicable dividend tax rate
□ Saved a copy of the form
□ Set reminder to update in 3 years
```

## 3. First ETF Purchase Checklist

### Preparation

```
□ Account has settled cash
□ ETF to purchase determined:
   □ VTI (total US market) — recommended
   □ VOO (S&P 500)
   □ VT (total world)
   □ Other: ________________
□ Know the current ETF price (approximately)
□ Know how much I want to invest: $________
```

### Purchase Process

```
□ Logged into brokerage account
□ Found ETF by ticker (e.g., VTI)
□ Clicked "Buy" / "Trade"
□ Selected order type:
   □ Market Order — recommended for beginners
   □ Limit Order — if you want a specific price
□ Entered quantity:
   □ In dollars (if fractional shares available)
   □ In shares
□ Reviewed order details
□ Confirmed purchase
□ Received execution confirmation
```

### Right After Purchase

```
□ Verified ETF appeared in portfolio
□ Checked purchase price and quantity
□ Took screenshot/note of first purchase (for memory!)
```

### Setting Up for the Future

```
□ Enabled DRIP (dividend reinvestment):
   □ Settings → Dividends → Reinvest
□ Set up automatic purchases (if available):
   □ Recurring Investment
   □ Amount: $_______
   □ Frequency: weekly / monthly
   □ Date: _____ of each month
□ Set up automatic bank transfer (if needed)
```

### What NOT to Do

```
□ DON'T check the price every hour
□ DON'T panic at 1-2% drops
□ DON'T sell in the first days/weeks
□ DON'T change strategy without good reason
□ DON'T tell everyone about your investments
```

## 4. Market Crash Checklist

> Use this checklist when the market drops 10%+
> and you feel panic

### Step 1: Stop and Breathe

```
□ Closed brokerage app
□ Took 10 deep breaths
□ Reminded myself: "This is temporary"
□ Re-read my investment plan
```

### Step 2: Reality Check

```
□ My investment horizon: ____ years
   → If > 5 years: the drop doesn't matter

□ Do I need this money in the next year?
   □ NO → Don't sell!
   □ YES → Why is it in stocks?

□ Have my life circumstances changed?
   □ NO → Stick to the plan
   □ YES → Reconsider (but not in panic!)
```

### Step 3: Historical Perspective

```
Remind yourself:
□ S&P 500 has dropped 20%+ many times
□ Every time the market recovered
□ Worst time to sell is at the bottom
□ Best market days often follow the worst
□ Investors who sold in 2008 missed 500%+ gains
```

### Step 4: What to Do

```
✅ DO:
□ Continue regular investments (DCA)
□ Consider rebalancing (buy stocks cheaper)
□ Use tax-loss harvesting (if applicable)
□ Talk to an investor friend (not a panicker)
□ Re-read stories of investors who survived crises

❌ DON'T:
□ Sell in panic
□ Try to time the bottom
□ Read panic-inducing news
□ Change strategy based on emotions
□ Check portfolio every day
```

### Step 5: Actions After the Drop

```
If drop > 10%:
□ Check if there are free funds to buy
□ Consider an extra purchase "on sale"

If drop > 20%:
□ Check stock/bond ratio
□ If bonds > target — rebalance
□ Write down thoughts and emotions (useful for future)

If drop > 30%:
□ This is rare! (2008, 2020)
□ All above actions +
□ Consider maximum IRA/401(k) contributions
□ Remember: these are the best buying opportunities
```

### Mantras for Crisis

```
Repeat when panicking:

"Time in market > timing the market"

"This drop is a discount on my future purchases"

"I'm investing for 10+ years, not 10 days"

"Selling now = locking in losses"

"Stay the course" (John Bogle)
```

## 5. Annual Investor Checklist

> Complete once a year (e.g., January or your birthday)

### Portfolio Review

```
□ Checked total portfolio value: $_________
□ Compared to last year: $_________ (+___%)
□ Checked total investments for the year: $_________
□ Checked dividends received: $_________
```

### Rebalancing

```
Current allocation:
□ US Stocks: _____%  (target: _____%)
□ International: _____%  (target: _____%)
□ Bonds: _____%  (target: _____%)
□ Other: _____%

□ Deviation from target > 5%?
   □ YES → Rebalance
   □ NO → Leave as is

□ If rebalancing:
   □ Sell: _________________ for $_______
   □ Buy: _________________ for $_______
   □ Or: direct new contributions to lagging class
```

### Strategy Review

```
□ Have my goals changed?
   □ NO → Continue
   □ YES → Adjust plan

□ Has my horizon changed?
   □ Became shorter → More bonds?
   □ Stayed the same → Continue

□ Has my risk tolerance changed?
   □ Panicked during drops → Fewer stocks
   □ Wanted more stocks during drops → More stocks
   □ Everything is fine → Continue
```

### Tax Optimization

```
□ Checked unrealized losses
   □ Have losses > $3,000 → Consider tax-loss harvesting
□ Checked unrealized gains
   □ Large gains → Don't sell unnecessarily
□ Maximized contributions to tax-advantaged accounts?
   □ 401(k): invested $_______ of $24,500 (2026)
      (+$8,000 catch-up at 50+, or +$11,250 at ages 60-63,
       which REPLACES the $8,000 rather than stacking on it)
   □ IRA: invested $_______ of $7,500 (2026) (+$1,100 at 50+)
   □ HSA (if applicable): invested $_______ of $4,400 self-only /
      $8,750 family (2026) (+$1,000 at 55+)
```

### Data Updates

```
□ Checked account beneficiaries
   □ Current after family changes
□ Updated contact information with broker
□ Checked W-8BEN form (for non-residents)
   □ Valid for ____ more years
   □ Need to update? □
```

### Planning for Next Year

```
□ New monthly investment goal: $_______
□ Planned major expenses: $_______
□ Need to adjust plan?
□ Automatic transfers set up?
□ Date of next annual review: ___________
```

## 6. Tax Filing Checklist (USA)

> For US residents. Complete January-April.

### Collecting Documents (January-February)

```
□ Waited for forms from broker (arrive by February 15):
   □ 1099-DIV (dividends)
   □ 1099-B (sales)
   □ 1099-INT (interest)
   □ Consolidated 1099

□ Collected IRA contribution data:
   □ Traditional IRA contributions: $_______
   □ Roth IRA contributions: $_______

□ Collected 401(k) data:
   □ W-2 form with contribution information
```

### Filing the Return (February-April)

```
□ Chose filing method:
   □ Self-file (TurboTax, H&R Block, FreeTaxUSA)
   □ With tax professional (CPA)

□ Reported investment income:
   □ Dividends (ordinary and qualified)
   □ Capital gains (short-term / long-term)
   □ Interest

□ Applied Traditional IRA contribution deduction (if eligible)
□ Reported foreign taxes (for Foreign Tax Credit)
□ Double-checked all numbers
```

### Optimization

```
□ Used tax-loss harvesting?
   □ Losses offset against gains
   □ Remaining losses (up to $3,000) against income

□ Checked qualified dividends
   □ Taxed at LTCG rates (0/15/20%)

□ Were all sales long-term (> 1 year)?
   □ If not — remember for the future
```

### Filing and Payment

```
□ Filed return by April 15
   □ Or extended to October 15 (Form 4868)
□ Paid taxes (if owed)
□ Received refund (if due)
□ Saved copy of return
□ Saved all supporting documents (7 years)
```

### For Non-Residents

```
□ W-8BEN form completed and valid
□ Dividend tax withheld at source
□ Checked tax treaty with your country
□ Reported income in your country (if required)
```
