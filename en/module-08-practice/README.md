## Lesson 8.1: Preparing to Invest

### Financial Checklist

```
Before you start investing, check:

☐ High-interest debts are paid off
   └── Credit cards, personal loans

☐ Emergency fund is created
   └── 3-6 months of expenses in savings account

☐ You have stable income
   └── Or another source for regular contributions

☐ Financial goals are defined
   └── Retirement, house, children's education

☐ You have basic knowledge (this course!)
   └── Understanding of risks and instruments

☐ Ready for long-term investing
   └── Minimum 5+ years, ideally 15+
```

### Defining Goals (SMART)

| Criterion | ❌ Bad Goal | ✅ Good Goal |
|-----------|------------|--------------|
| **S**pecific | "I want to be rich" | "Accumulate $500,000 for retirement" |
| **M**easurable | "I want more money" | "$1,000,000 by age 60" |
| **A**chievable | "$10M in 5 years with $1,000" | "$100k in 10 years at $500/mo" |
| **R**elevant | Disconnected from life | Connected to priorities |
| **T**ime-bound | "Someday" | "By 2045" |

### Calculating Required Investments

```
Future Value Formula:

FV = PMT × [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r]

Where:
FV — future value
PMT — monthly contribution
r — monthly return rate
n — number of months

Example:
Goal: $1,000,000 in 30 years
Return: 7% annually (0.58% monthly)

$1,000,000 = PMT × [((1.0058)^360 - 1) / 0.0058]
$1,000,000 = PMT × 1,219.97
PMT = $820/month

You need to invest ~$820/month!
```

## Lesson 8.3: Your First Stock Purchase

### ⚙️ Important Settings BEFORE Your First Purchase

**Cost Basis Method:**

```
Methods:
├── FIFO (First In, First Out) — default
│   └── Oldest shares sold first
├── LIFO (Last In, First Out)
│   └── Newest shares sold first
├── SpecID (Specific Identification) — RECOMMENDED
│   └── You choose specific lots when selling
└── Average Cost — mutual funds only

Why SpecID is better:
├── Can select lots with maximum loss (TLH)
├── Can select lots with minimum gain
└── Maximum control over taxes
```

> 💡 **Action:** Go to Settings → Tax Lot Method → select "Specific Identification"

**DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plan):**

```
Enable DRIP?

✅ Pros:
├── Automatic compound growth
├── No need to remember to reinvest
└── Fractional share purchases

⚠️ Cons in Taxable Account:
├── Dividends are taxed (even when reinvested!)
├── Many small lots complicate tracking
├── Harder to do Tax-Loss Harvesting
└── Many lines on 1099-B

Recommendation:
├── In IRA/401(k): enable DRIP ✅
└── In Taxable: consider manual reinvestment
```

> 💡 **Action:** Check DRIP settings in Dividends → Reinvestment

### Step-by-Step Instructions

```
1. Log into your brokerage account

2. Find the asset you want
   ├── Enter ticker (e.g., VTI)
   └── Or name (Vanguard Total Stock)

3. Click "Trade" or "Buy"

4. Fill out the order:
   ├── Action: Buy
   ├── Quantity: number of shares OR dollar amount
   ├── Order Type: Limit (recommended) — see below
   ├── Time in Force: Day (order valid for one day)
   └── Account: select the right account

   💡 Order Type:
   ├── Limit: set price 0.1-0.2% above current
   │   └── Protection from poor execution
   ├── Market: only for VOO/VTI/SPY mid-day
   │   └── Avoid at open (9:30) and close (4:00)
   └── Why Limit is better: during volatility, Market
       orders can execute at unfavorable prices

5. Review details
   ├── Total cost
   ├── Commission (should be $0 for most)
   └── Estimated price

6. Confirm order
   └── "Review" → "Submit"

7. Check execution
   ├── For Market Order — instant
   └── For Limit Order — may take time
```

### Purchase Example

```
You want to invest $5,000 in VTI

Let's say VTI trades at $250

Option 1: Buy whole shares
├── $5,000 / $250 = 20 shares
├── Remaining: $0
└── Works for most brokers

Option 2: Fractional shares
├── Fidelity, Schwab: can buy exactly $5,000
├── You'll get: 20.00 shares
└── Entire amount invested
```

### What to Do After Purchase

```
✓ Check position in your portfolio
✓ Set up DRIP (dividend reinvestment)
✓ Record purchase for your records
✓ Set reminder for next purchase

✗ DON'T check the price every hour
✗ DON'T panic at small fluctuations
✗ DON'T sell at the first drop
```

## Lesson 8.5: Monitoring and Adjustment

### Regular Actions

```
Monthly:
├── Regular investment (automatic)
└── Quick check that everything works

Quarterly:
├── Check asset allocation
├── Assess if rebalancing needed
└── Tax planning (for taxable account)

Annually:
├── Full portfolio review
├── Rebalance if needed
├── Assess progress toward goals
├── Adjust contributions
└── Tax reporting
```

### When to Rebalance

```
Triggers for rebalancing:

1. Deviation more than 5% from target
   Example: target 70/30, became 78/22 → rebalance

2. One year since last rebalancing

3. Significant life situation change
   ├── Job change
   ├── Marriage/divorce
   ├── Birth of child
   └── Approaching retirement
```

### Tracking Results

```
What to track:
├── Total portfolio value
├── Returns (for year, since inception)
├── Comparison to benchmark
├── Dividends received
└── Progress toward goals

Tools:
├── Broker app
├── Empower (free) — empower.com
├── Google Sheets / Excel
└── Specialized apps

⚠️ Don't obsess over short-term results!
```

## Lesson 8.7: How to Read Tax Forms

### Form 1099-DIV (Dividends)

```
Received in January-February for the previous year

Key fields:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Box 1a: Total ordinary dividends                │
│         Total dividend amount                   │
│         → Taxed as ordinary income              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 1b: Qualified dividends                     │
│         Qualified dividends                     │
│         → Preferential rate 0/15/20%            │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 2a: Total capital gain distributions        │
│         Capital gain distributions              │
│         → Long-term rate                        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 4: Federal income tax withheld              │
│        Withheld federal tax                     │
│        → Usually $0 for US residents            │
│        → For non-residents: 15-30%              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 7: Foreign tax paid                         │
│        Foreign tax (for VXUS, etc.)             │
│        → Can claim tax credit                   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Example:
Box 1a: $1,500 (total dividends)
Box 1b: $1,200 (of which qualified)
Box 7: $45 (foreign tax)

Tax: $1,200 × 15% = $180, minus $45 credit = $135
Remaining $300 × your ordinary rate
```

### Form 1099-B (Securities Sales)

```
Received if you sold stocks/ETFs

Key fields:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Box 1d: Proceeds (sale proceeds)                │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 1e: Cost basis (purchase cost)              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 1g: Gain/loss                               │
│         = Proceeds - Cost basis                 │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 1c: Date acquired (purchase date)           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Box 1b: Date sold (sale date)                   │
│         If held >1 year = long-term             │
│         If held <1 year = short-term            │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Example:
Bought VTI for $5,000 in January 2023
Sold for $6,500 in March 2025 (>1 year)

Box 1d: $6,500 (proceeds)
Box 1e: $5,000 (cost basis)
Box 1g: $1,500 (long-term gain)

Tax: $1,500 × 15% = $225 (if income $49,451-$545,500, single, 2026)
```

### Brokerage Statement

```
What to look for in monthly/quarterly statement:

Account Summary:
├── Beginning value — value at period start
├── Ending value — value at period end
├── Net change — change (profit/loss)
└── Deposits/Withdrawals — additions/removals

Holdings (Positions):
├── Symbol — asset ticker
├── Quantity — number of shares
├── Price — current price
├── Market value — market value
├── Cost basis — purchase cost
├── Unrealized G/L — unrealized profit/loss
└── % of portfolio — portfolio percentage

Income:
├── Dividends — dividends received
├── Interest — interest
└── Distributions — distributions

Activity:
├── Buys — purchases
├── Sells — sales
├── Dividends received — dividends received
└── Dividend reinvestment — reinvestment
```

### Tax Calculation Example

```
During the year you received:
├── Qualified dividends: $2,000
├── Non-qualified dividends: $300
├── Long-term capital gain: $1,500
├── Short-term capital gain: $200
└── Foreign tax paid: $60

Income: $80,000 (22% ordinary rate, 15% preferential)

Calculation:
Qualified dividends: $2,000 × 15% = $300
Non-qualified: $300 × 22% = $66
Long-term gain: $1,500 × 15% = $225
Short-term gain: $200 × 22% = $44

Total before credits: $635
Minus Foreign Tax Credit: $60
Tax owed: $575
```

## Lesson 8.9: Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

### Top 10 Beginner Mistakes

```
1. Investing without an emergency fund
   ✗ Will have to sell at a bad time
   ✓ First 3-6 months of expenses in cash

2. Trying to time the market
   ✗ "I'll wait until it drops"
   ✓ Invest regularly, time in market matters more

3. Lack of diversification
   ✗ Everything in one stock or sector
   ✓ Broad index ETFs

4. Panic selling during drops
   ✗ Selling at the bottom
   ✓ Follow your plan, it's an opportunity to buy more

5. Chasing returns
   ✗ "This stock went up 500%!"
   ✓ Past returns don't guarantee future performance

6. Trading too frequently
   ✗ Constant buying/selling
   ✓ Commissions and taxes eat into profits

7. Ignoring fees
   ✗ Expensive actively managed funds
   ✓ Expense ratio matters enormously

8. No plan
   ✗ Emotional decisions
   ✓ Written plan and discipline

9. Trying to beat the market
   ✗ Stock picking without experience
   ✓ Index investing

10. Delaying the start
    ✗ "I'll start when I have more money"
    ✓ Time is the main factor, start now!
```

<details>
<summary><b>Question 8:</b> What's the difference between NYSE and NASDAQ?</summary>

**Answer:**
- NYSE — oldest exchange (1792), physical trading floor, largest traditional companies
- NASDAQ — electronic exchange (1971), focus on technology companies
- Both are regulated by the SEC and equally reliable

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 9:</b> What is the S&P 500?</summary>

**Answer:** An index of the 500 largest US public companies by market capitalization. Covers ~80% of the US market. Main benchmark for evaluating investment results.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 10:</b> What's the difference between a stock and a bond?</summary>

**Answer:**
- Stock = ownership share in a company. High risk, high potential return.
- Bond = debt. You lend money and receive interest. Lower risk, lower return.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 11:</b> What is an ETF and why is it popular?</summary>

**Answer:** Exchange-Traded Fund — a fund that trades on an exchange like a stock. Popular because of: low fees (from 0.03%), instant diversification, liquidity, tax efficiency.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 12:</b> What does Expense Ratio 0.03% mean?</summary>

**Answer:** Annual fund fee. 0.03% = $3 per year for every $10,000 invested. Automatically deducted from the fund's value.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 13:</b> What's the difference between Traditional IRA and Roth IRA?</summary>

**Answer:**
- Traditional IRA: contributions reduce taxes now, you pay tax on withdrawal
- Roth IRA: contributions from after-tax income, withdrawal is tax-free
- Roth is better if you expect higher taxes in the future

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 14:</b> What is the IRA contribution limit for 2026?</summary>

**Answer:** $7,500 per year ($8,600 for age 50+). The limit is combined for Traditional and Roth IRA.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 15:</b> What's the advantage of 401(k) over IRA?</summary>

**Answer:**
- Higher limit: $24,500 per year (vs $7,500 for IRA)
- Employer matching — free money from employer
- Automatic payroll deductions

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 16:</b> What is the tax rate on qualified dividends?</summary>

**Answer:** 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income. For most people — 15%. This is lower than the ordinary income tax rate.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 17:</b> What is Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)?</summary>

**Answer:** Regular investing of a fixed amount regardless of market price. Reduces the risk of buying at the peak. Example: $500 every month.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 18:</b> Why is passive investing usually better than active?</summary>

**Answer:** ~90% of active funds underperform the index over 15+ years. Reasons: high fees, trading costs, taxes, human errors.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 19:</b> What's the difference between VTI and VOO?</summary>

**Answer:**
- VTI — entire US market (~3,500 companies), including small/mid-cap
- VOO — only S&P 500 (500 largest)
- Returns are very similar, VTI has slightly more diversification

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 20:</b> When should you rebalance your portfolio?</summary>

**Answer:**
1. When deviation exceeds 5% from target allocation
2. Once a year by calendar
3. When there are significant life situation changes

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 21:</b> What is sequence of returns risk?</summary>

**Answer:** The risk of getting low returns in the first years after retirement. A market drop during active withdrawals can deplete the portfolio faster.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 22:</b> What is FOMO and why is it dangerous?</summary>

**Answer:** Fear Of Missing Out — fear of missing profits. Dangerous because it makes you buy at the peak after a rise, ignoring risks.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 23:</b> How does the Rule of 72 help investors?</summary>

**Answer:** 72 ÷ return = years to double capital. Example: 72 ÷ 7% = ~10 years. Helps quickly estimate investment growth.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 24:</b> What is W-8BEN form and who needs it?</summary>

**Answer:** A form for non-US residents to reduce dividend tax (usually from 30% to 10-15% under tax treaty). Valid for 3 years.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 25:</b> What is DRIP?</summary>

**Answer:** Dividend Reinvestment Plan — automatic reinvestment of dividends into purchasing additional shares. Increases the compound interest effect.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 26:</b> What is the maximum loss you can deduct from taxes annually?</summary>

**Answer:** $3,000 net investment losses can be deducted against ordinary income. The remainder carries forward to future years.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 27:</b> What is the wash sale rule?</summary>

**Answer:** A rule prohibiting loss deduction if you bought the same or substantially identical asset within 30 days before or after the sale.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 28:</b> How many ETFs are enough for a diversified portfolio?</summary>

**Answer:** 2-4 ETFs. For example: VTI (US stocks) + VXUS (international) + BND (bonds). Or simply VT (whole world).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 29:</b> What is the 4% rule?</summary>

**Answer:** Safe withdrawal rule: you can withdraw 4% of your portfolio annually with high probability of not depleting funds over 30 years. $1M × 4% = $40,000/year.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Question 30:</b> Why is "time in the market" more important than "timing the market"?</summary>

**Answer:** Missing even a few of the market's best days drastically reduces returns. It's impossible to predict those days. Staying invested consistently is statistically more profitable.

</details>
