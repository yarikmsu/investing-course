## 🔴 The 4 critical issues

**C33. 📒 CPA — module-04-brokers-taxes/README.md, module-04-brokers-taxes/README.md** (RU+EN)  
The module recommends Backdoor Roth but omits the single most important CPA trap: the IRC §408(d)(2) pro-rata aggregation rule. Any existing PRE-TAX balance in ANY traditional/SEP/SIMPLE IRA (aggregated across all the taxpayer's IRAs on Dec 31) makes the conversion mostly taxable — the $7,500 nondeductible contribution is NOT converted tax-free if there are pre-tax IRA dollars. There is no mention of Form 8606 (required to report the nondeductible basis) either. A reader following this advice with a rollover IRA balance will get an unexpected tax bill.  
→ *Fix:* Add a warning box: backdoor Roth is only clean if you have $0 pre-tax balance across ALL traditional/SEP/SIMPLE IRAs (the pro-rata rule aggregates them at year-end). Note Form 8606 must be filed for the nondeductible contribution and the conversion. Mention rolling pre-tax IRA into an employer 401(k) first to isolate basis. Mirror in EN line 517.

**C79. 🏛️ IRS Examiner — NON-RESIDENTS.md, NON-RESIDENTS.md** (RU+EN)  
The single most dangerous cross-border trap is entirely absent: PFIC (Passive Foreign Investment Company) treatment under IRC §1297. The course aggressively recommends Irish/Luxembourg UCITS ETFs (CSPX, VWRA, EIMI) to non-residents, AND has a dedicated 'What if I become a US resident?' section. But ANY non-US fund (every UCITS ETF) is a PFIC for a US person. A reader who follows the course's advice, builds a UCITS portfolio, then moves to the US (gets a Green Card / passes Substantial Presence) is immediately exposed to punitive Section 1291 excess-distribution tax (highest ordinary rate + compounding interest charge on deferred gain) plus annual Form 8621 filing. This is the exact 'UCITS good for NRA / toxic for US person' matrix that must be explicit. The course currently leads US-bound readers straight into the trap without a single warning.  
→ *Fix:* Add a prominent boxed warning in both the Irish ETF section and the 'What if I become a US resident?' Q&A: Irish/Luxembourg UCITS ETFs are PFICs for US tax persons. If you ever become a US tax resident (Green Card or Substantial Presence Test), UCITS holdings trigger punitive PFIC taxation (Form 8621, Section 1291 excess-distribution regime, no LTCG rates, interest charge) — and a QEF/MTM election is usually impossible because UCITS funds don't provide the required US PFIC Annual Information Statement. Advise anyone with a realistic chance of moving to the US to either hold US-domiciled ETFs or sell UCITS holdings BEFORE establishing US residency, and to consult a cross-border tax advisor before relocating.

**C87. 🏛️ IRS Examiner — ADVANCED.md, ADVANCED.md** (RU+EN)  
The Backdoor Roth and non-deductible Traditional IRA contribution are taught with zero mention of Form 8606. Form 8606 is MANDATORY: it reports the non-deductible contribution (Part I) and the conversion (Part II) and is the only way the IRS knows the basis is after-tax. Without an 8606 on file, the IRS treats the entire conversion as taxable (double taxation of the same dollars) and a missing/late 8606 carries a $50 penalty per form plus loss of basis. This is the single highest audit-exposure omission in the advanced tax section.  
→ *Fix:* Add a mandatory-filing callout: every non-deductible Traditional IRA contribution AND every Roth conversion must be reported on Form 8606 for that tax year (Part I tracks basis, Part II reports the conversion). Note that basis carries forward year to year on line 14 and that a forgotten 8606 should be filed retroactively. Mega Backdoor (after-tax 401k) basis is tracked by the plan, not 8606 — clarify the distinction.

**C96. 🏛️ IRS Examiner — RUSSIA.md** (RU only)  
The file has ZERO coverage of the US estate-tax trap for non-resident aliens (NRAs), which VERIFIED-FACTS.md identifies as the single highest-value cross-border issue for this audience. A Russia-resident NRA who dies holding US-domiciled ETFs/stocks (VOO, VTI, VT, BND, QQQ) is subject to US estate tax at up to 40% on US-situs assets above only a $60,000 filing threshold (unified credit $13,000), with Form 706-NA required. Critically, the US-Russia treaty was an INCOME-tax treaty only — there was never a US-Russia ESTATE-tax treaty — so this $60k exposure existed before the suspension and remains fully in force. For an audience steered toward US brokers (IBKR) and US ETFs, omitting a 40% wealth-confiscation risk above $60k is a severe, audit-grade gap.  
→ *Fix:* Add a dedicated section: NRA US estate tax. State the $60,000 US-situs threshold, top rate 40%, that US-domiciled ETFs/stocks are US-situs (IRC §2104) even when held via a foreign broker, that no US-Russia estate treaty exists (so no relief, suspension irrelevant here), Form 706-NA filing requirement, and that Irish-domiciled (UCITS) accumulating ETFs via IBKR are non-US-situs and sit outside US estate tax. Cross-reference VERIFIED-FACTS.md estate-tax rows.
